# Motion and visual effects

Choose motion according to the function of the slide, audience and platform.

| Purpose | Treatment | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open a story or mark a section | One slow opacity or slight scale change on a large image | Keep the title readable from arrival. |
| Explain a process | Reveal nodes and their labels together, then trace the connecting path | Use the sequence to guide explanation. Stop on the complete diagram. |
| Walk through a comparison | Reveal related groups in the order the audience should read | Keep shared units and baselines visible during the comparison. |
| Present an analytical or executive result | Short fades or small translations | Let the numbers become still quickly. |
| Host a playful workshop | A single lively easing curve or rhythmic entry | Put movement in the prompt or transition and keep instructions stable. |
| Demonstrate a technical system | Use a grid reveal or restrained glow around the point of interest | Avoid making labels flicker. Keep moving background effects off reading slides. |
| Show an editorial story | Stagger the image, heading and caption by meaning | Move the fewest elements needed to establish a focal point. |

Start from opacity and transform. Use a modest offset and an automatic lead-in sequence with no per-bullet clicks. Use masks, gradients, an atmospheric graphic layer or a subtle highlight sweep only when they serve the story. Use progress, clickable slides or a deliberate interaction when it helps the speaker control the presentation.

Honor reduced motion and present the full content in print. In PowerPoint use native entrances or transitions for the intended effect. Reserve a fixed stage and manual slide advance unless timed playback is requested. On small screens test controls and avoid heavy effects. Troubleshoot by checking the active state, fonts and CSS, rather than increasing motion or adding viewport-based layout rules.
