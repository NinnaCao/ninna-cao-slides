# Examples of adapting the visual direction

Use these examples to match composition and evidence to the current brief. Choose colors, type and media from the project context.

## Research readout

**Purpose.** Help a team decide what to test after observing where people get stuck.

| Slide idea | Visual and context |
| --- | --- |
| Start where people pause | Show a project photo of a participant doing the task. Reserve a clear text area for the question that prompted the observation. Caption what the team actually saw. |
| Turn an observation into a test | Draw three connected, large labels: “Watch the task,” “State the need,” and “Test one idea.” Under each label, add the activity and its output. Reveal the groups as the speaker explains them. |
| Use the smallest useful prototype | Show a real rough sketch next to one distinct interactive state. Name the question each can answer. |
| Decide what to do next | Put the evidence, remaining uncertainty and next decision together. Place supporting records after an appendix divider. |

A quiet photo-led treatment can work well for a small-room readout. Blend a scene behind the opening if the subject stays clear. Put observation and prototype evidence in crisp frames when the audience needs to inspect it.

## Live workshop

**Purpose.** Help a room of participants choose one idea to test together.

| Slide idea | Visual and context |
| --- | --- |
| Define what a successful session produces | Use one large headline and a short target output. Pair it with a photograph of the activity when available. |
| Group ideas by need | Show a legible sticky-note grouping or a clean redraw. Explain the categories and give a concrete example of moving a note. |
| Choose the uncertainty | Draw a simple need → question → evidence sequence. Give each label enough space to read from the back of a room. |
| Give the group its next action | Put the instruction, time and expected output on the same slide. Keep optional prompts in the appendix. |

A bright, high-contrast palette with strong sans-serif type works in a well-lit room. Use an alternative direction if the brand, references or space point to one.

## Product or marketing talk in a dim room

**Purpose.** Explain how a team turns observation into a product decision, with energy, for an audience watching a projector. See `assets/example-cinematic-deck.json` (Cinematic Night).

| Slide idea | Visual and context |
| --- | --- |
| Cover | A huge condensed title on the left; a dark product or scene photo on the right fading into the stage. Presenter and date in mono below. |
| Agenda | Three image tiles, one per part, with big accent numerals and one line each. |
| Section divider | Outlined numeral, a short title, a photograph fading in from the right and four marks along the bottom. |
| Steps | Photo plate fading from the top right; four cards with mono labels such as "01 · ARRIVE". |
| Chain or framework | An SVG diagram that traces in, with the final stage in the accent colour, and one rule-of-thumb line. |
| Targets | A paper slide: big numbers beside a white-background product shot blended with `multiply`. |
| Close | A statement title over a picture fading from the right, three takeaways. |
| Sources | Generated from the credits ledger. |
